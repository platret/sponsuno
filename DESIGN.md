# DESIGN.md – Sponsuno visual system

The source of truth for colours and fonts is the **Sponsuno Corporate Design Manual v1.0 (November 2026)**. This file translates it into how the website uses them. It was rewritten in November 2026 after a research round (Mobbin heroes and landing pages, Awwwards winners, a palette analysis and an audit of the old page). If the code and this file disagree, fix one of them in the same change.

Tokens are CSS custom properties in [`src/styles/tokens.css`](src/styles/tokens.css); the landing page base layer is [`src/styles/site.css`](src/styles/site.css); each section has its own stylesheet in `src/styles/sections/`.

## Principles

1. **Calm, clear, credible.** The page must work for a 22-year-old athlete, a volunteer club board and an SME owner. Clarity first, then atmosphere.
2. **Calm motion that leads into the content.** Since November 2026 the page moves with the scroll: sections rise in, photos and frames open as you scroll into them, the hero recedes as the page moves on. Every movement is scroll-linked or tied to a scroll step, small and finished by the time an element is comfortably in view; nothing bounces, spins or types. The thin gold line (the hero start frame and the scroll story, where messy papers pass it and come out as ordered pairs) is the page's signature and echoes in the steps line.
3. **Light page, navy frame, gold accent.** Light surfaces carry about 75% of the page. Navy is used for the hero plus the scroll story under it (see Layout → Navy share) and the closing waitlist + footer block. Gold is a flat accent (about 5%): never a surface, never a gradient, never a glow.
4. **Show the product, not decoration.** Product UI (the match card, the KI-Manager draft) explains Sponsuno better than abstract visuals.
5. **Honest content in honest form.** Fictional examples, AI images and stock photos are labelled (stock photos as "Symbolbild"). No fake logos, numbers or testimonials.

What we removed and should not bring back: WebGL shaders, the 3D emblem, tilted marquees, gold liquid sections, magnetic buttons, card tilt and glare, shine sweeps, rotating borders, scroll hijacking (Lenis), smooth-scroll overrides, glass navigation.

Rules: Cormorant never below about 28px and never for UI or body text. Sentence case, no all-caps labels, no letter-spaced eyebrows. Lining figures for numerals. Fonts come from `@fontsource` (Latin subset), not from Google's servers.

## Motion

Plan agreed on 2026-11-03 (replaces the old "one signature moment" budget). CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: view()`) as progressive enhancement; no animation or scroll library, no scroll hijacking, no smooth-scroll override, no JS per scroll frame.

**Gate.** `index.html` sets `html.motion` unless the visitor prefers reduced motion, and `html.scroll-fx` when motion is on and the browser supports `animation-timeline: view()` (Chrome, Edge, Safari 26). The primitives in `site.css` are additionally wrapped in `@supports` and `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. Section-specific scroll rules are written under `.scroll-fx`.
- Without `.scroll-fx` but with `.motion` (Firefox): elements keep the `data-reveal` fade (IntersectionObserver, opacity + 12 px, 0.6 s, once). Frames, photos and lines are simply static.
- Reduced motion, no JS, prerendered HTML: final state, nothing moves. Reduced motion switched on mid-visit also drops all `.scroll-fx` animations.

**Rules.** Animate only `opacity`, `translate`, `scale` (individual transform properties) and `clip-path`; no colour, size, blur or filter animation. Scroll-linked progress is linear; time-based motion uses `--ease-out`. UI scales at most 0.92 → 1 (icons and portraits down to 0.86, the KI device frame 0.8 on desktop); photos 1.18 → 1 inside their frame. Dark surfaces (the KI device) and things inside a block that already fades (icons, portraits) scale without their own fade (`sfx-scale`): stacked or partial opacity turns navy into grey. Opacity reaches 1 at 60 % of an element's range, the motion ends after `--scroll-dist` (40 vh, 32 vh on phones) of scroll from the moment its top enters, i.e. when its top sits at about 60 % of the viewport. Nothing above the fold at scroll 0 is hidden (the hero only recedes on the way out, the proof line is static). Pinned budget: at most two, each at most one extra viewport, none below 600 px. Used: none at the moment; the KI-Manager bento's pin (≥ 961 px wide and ≥ 700 px tall, 50 vh hold, see Per section) is dormant on its static page; the sticky FAQ title and the sticky story stage are plain sticky layout beside (or above) flowing text, not a hold (see Story). No layout shift: only transforms, opacity and clips change. `will-change` only on the two signature elements if profiling asks for it.

**Details that keep it honest.**
- Timelines are measured against the real viewport: the default is `view(block 0px)` and every named `view-timeline` sets `view-timeline-inset: 0px` (the `auto` inset would take `scroll-padding`, i.e. the header on top and the sticky bar on phones, and shift every range; on viewports taller than the 940 px hero cap the hero would load already receded).
- Focus guard (`site.css`): `.scroll-fx [data-scroll]:focus-within { animation-name: none }`. A block holding focus shows its final state at once, so keyboard focus never lands on half-faded text or a faded focus ring.
- Tall viewports: where a section can be in view at scroll 0 (portrait tablets and monitors), its scroll animations are switched off by `min-height` media queries (`audience.css` 1140/1340/1700 px), so nothing visible at load starts hidden.
- Forced colours: the drawn hairlines (`::before` in Nicht nur Geld, Team) are hidden, the real border shows; the story graphic is hidden and the steps read as a normal section.
- Compositing: `opacity`, `translate` and `scale` run on the compositor; never put them on an SVG element (Chrome does not composite individual transforms on SVG), wrap it in a `span` (support icons, price markers). `clip-path` animations (photo frames, the waitlist surface, the check-mark wipe) repaint on the main thread by design; they are few and short (profiled smooth at 4× CPU throttling).

### Primitives (`site.css`, tokens in `tokens.css`)

Put `data-scroll="…"` on an element; keep `data-reveal=""` next to it where a fallback fade is wanted. One value per element, never on an element with its own CSS animation (wrap it). All primitive selectors have zero specificity, so a section class overrides them.

| `data-scroll` | Does | Default range | Tune with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rise` | opacity 0 → 1, `translate` from (`--scroll-x`, `--scroll-y`) to 0 | `cover 0` → `cover --scroll-dist` | `--scroll-x` (0), `--scroll-y` (40 px, 24 px on phones) |
| `fade` | opacity 0 → 1 | same | |
| `zoom-in` | opacity 0 → 1, `scale` `--scroll-zoom-from` → 1, `translate` `--scroll-zoom-y` → 0, origin 50 % 60 % | same | `--scroll-zoom-from` (.92), `--scroll-zoom-y` (16 px) |
| `draw-x` / `draw-y` | a line grows: `scale` 0 → 1 from the left / top | same | |
| `photo` | on the frame around an `img`/`picture`/`video`: frame clip `inset(--scroll-photo-inset round r)` → `inset(0 round r)`, image `scale` `--scroll-photo-from` → 1; sets `overflow: clip` and the radius | `cover 0` → `cover --scroll-dist-long` | `--scroll-photo-from` (1.18, 1.12 on phones), `--scroll-photo-inset` (4 %; any `inset()` offsets, e.g. `6% 8%`), `--scroll-photo-radius` (16 px) |
| `frame` | a surface opens: `clip-path: inset(--scroll-frame-inset round --scroll-frame-radius)` → `inset(0 round --scroll-frame-radius-end)` | `entry 0%` → `entry min(100%, --scroll-dist-long)` (final once fully in view) | `--scroll-frame-inset` (`0 clamp(.5rem, 3vw, 3rem)`, always smaller than the gutter), `--scroll-frame-radius` (28 px, 20 px on phones), `--scroll-frame-radius-end` (0) |
| `recede` | to opacity `--scroll-recede-opacity`, `translate` `--scroll-recede-y`, `scale` `--scroll-recede-scale` while scrolling out; rest state = start | `exit 0%` → `exit 100%` | `--scroll-recede-y` (−6 vh), `--scroll-recede-scale` (1), `--scroll-recede-opacity` (.4) |
| `group` | no animation; under `.scroll-fx` its `data-reveal` fade is switched off because its children animate | – | |

Shared knobs: `--scroll-timeline` (default `view()`; set it to a named `view-timeline` such as `--hero` to drive children from a section), `--scroll-range-start` / `--scroll-range-end` (any `animation-range` value; they inherit, so set them on the element unless the whole container should share them), `--scroll-i` (stagger index; each step adds `--scroll-stagger`, 4 vh, 0 on phones). `data-scroll-stagger` on a parent numbers its children 0–5. Keyframes `sfx-rise`, `sfx-fade`, `sfx-zoom`, `sfx-scale` (zoom without the fade, same knobs), `sfx-photo`, `sfx-photo-frame`, `sfx-frame`, `sfx-draw-x`, `sfx-draw-y`, `sfx-recede` may be reused in section CSS with own timelines (for pseudo-elements: name a `view-timeline` on the element and point the pseudo-element at it).

On short stages the pairs, the legend and the match card scale down (container queries on the stage height: .88 below 600 px, .72 below 500 px); on phones and tablets the pairs then widen by the same factor, so they fill the window right of the line and the names fit. - **Layout**: desktop (≥ 961 px) text left (`clamp(19rem, 29vw, 27rem)`), sticky stage right, bleeding to the viewport edge, height `clamp(420px, 100svh − header − 2rem, 640px)`, centred vertically; steps at least 44 svh; reading line at 50 %. Phones and tablets: the heading scrolls normally, then the stage sticks under the header (45 svh, 290–420 px; tablets cap the composition at 640 px, centred; tablets at least 960 px tall get 52 svh, 420–540 px, so the pairs stay at .88 instead of .72), the steps scroll up beneath it (2.5 rem navy fade under the stage edge, ending above the reading line at 66 %); the caption is a bar on the stage's bottom edge. Seeker cards are 210 px (200 px at .74 on phones, .7 on stages under 360 px wide, so their text stays left of the line). On phones the column heads wrap to two lines ("Sucht / Unterstützung"), so they stay left of the line.
- **Technique**: two IntersectionObservers (a 2 % band at the reading line; the steps and the section entering or leaving the screen) trigger one position check: the current step is the last one whose top has passed the reading line, else the first. So a reload, Back, a Home/End or logo jump never leaves a stale picture, and nothing runs per scroll frame. A focused link inside a step forces its state. Item positions per state and layout come from the table in `lib/storyLayout.ts`, rendered as a `<style>` in the prerendered HTML (CLS 0). Only `translate`, `opacity`, `scale` change, with CSS transitions under `.motion` (.8 s translate, .45 s opacity, staggered 60–120 ms); a fast flick lands on the last state. Tilt is static; raw and clean faces cross-fade. Two clip windows meet at the line, so things vanish into it and come out of it (the scan). No scroll handler, no hijacking, no library.
- **Inactive steps**: only their headings dim (opacity .6) and the label rule (.35), with motion only; body text and labels keep full contrast (WCAG 1.4.3); `:focus-within` restores everything. On phones and tablets the links in the steps get a scroll margin of the stage height plus its soft edge, so a link reached with Tab scrolls in below the sticky stage, not behind it (WCAG 2.4.11).
- **Fallbacks**: no JS, screens up to 560 px tall, and phones with reduced motion get the snapshot sequence: each step shows its own static picture beside (desktop) or above (phones) its text (`clamp(340px, 100svh − header − 3rem, 600px)` on desktop, 390 px on phones; `content-visibility: auto`), nothing hidden, full contrast. The pinned layout is gated on `.js` alone (set before first paint), so the 4 s reveal failsafe never flips the layout; if the app never starts, the stage stays on `heute` beside fully readable text. Reduced motion on larger screens: the stage switches states without transitions. Forced colours and print: the picture is hidden. The snapshots are always in the prerendered HTML; once the page has hydrated, they are removed while the stage is pinned (they are `display: none` there, so nothing moves) and rendered again if the screen changes to a snapshot layout (rotation, reduced motion switched on).
- **Fonts and CLS**: metric-matched fallback faces (`Manrope Fallback` on Arial, `Cormorant Fallback` on Times New Roman, `tokens.css`) keep the font swap from moving anything; Manrope 400/600 and Cormorant 600 are preloaded, so on a slow phone the swap mostly happens with or shortly after the first paint; the hero badge reserves two lines on phones. The H1 and the lead only rise on load (no opacity start), so the LCP paints at once.
- **Honesty**: every name is generic and fictional (regions are real), the stock photo in the match card stays "Symbolbild", the KI parts are labelled "Geplant" / "in Entwicklung". The graphic is `aria-hidden`; its text alternative (`story.artAlt`) is given once, in the hero.

## Imagery

- Landing page: free stock photos (Pexels licence or Unsplash licence only), in `src/assets/photos/`: the athlete avatar in the match card of the scroll story and the four tiles of "Für wen Sponsuno gedacht ist". They are always labelled "Symbolbild" (match card label; a note under the tiles) and never presented as Sponsuno users, partners or customers. Every one has an entry in `photoCredits` (content.ts), which the Impressum lists.
- Choosing stock photos: real people in a Swiss, Alpine or plainly European setting, natural light, not posed-corporate. No legible brand logos or sponsor prints, no recognisable celebrities, no children as main subject. Prefer views where the people are not the point (backs, side views, hands at work).
- One grade for the set: after cropping, the audience tiles get one shared grade (saturation evened out, levels 3–98 %, a slight warm tint in the highlights and a navy tint in the shadows) so the four read as one series. Keep the grade when a tile is replaced.
- Preparing them: crop and resize with `sips` to at most twice the display size (tiles 640 × 480, match avatar 104 × 104 for its 52 px circle), JPEG quality about 58–78, strip metadata (`jpegtran -copy none -optimize -progressive`), aim for under 100 KB each. Originals stay outside the repo (`../photo-originals/`). Every `<img>` gets `width`/`height`, a German `alt` that describes the picture, `decoding="async"` and `loading="lazy"` below the fold.
- Demo: the AI-generated persona portrait (`src/assets/symbolbild-mtb-192.jpg`) stays there, always labelled "KI-generiertes Symbolbild".
- Founders: no photos yet, monograms instead (see Layout → Team). Only real, consented photos of the four founders may replace them; never stock or generated faces.
- Next step: a consented photo day (athletes, a sport club, a culture club, a local business owner, the founders, two or three places in the region), graded consistently (warm highlights, navy-leaning shadows). Those photos then replace the stock Symbolbilder.
